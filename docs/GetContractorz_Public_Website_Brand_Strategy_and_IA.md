# GetContractorz Public Website Brand, Positioning, Information Architecture & Copywriting Foundation

**Document type:** Brand and public-website strategy specification  
**Primary purpose:** Source of truth for conversion copywriting, SEO planning, public-page architecture, and disciplined brand communication  
**Scope:** Public website only  
**Dashboard/product application:** Out of scope; existing role-based dashboard functionality remains unchanged  
**Current market:** Canada and the United States  
**Status:** Working foundation based on the verified Product & Messaging Foundation and the existing Public Website Design System  
**Copy policy:** This document defines what the website must communicate and how pages should be structured. It intentionally does **not** provide final page copy.

---

## 0. How to use this document

This document should be used before writing or rebuilding any GetContractorz public page. It combines the verified product facts, business positioning, trust boundaries, design language, conversion strategy, SEO structure, and page inventory into one operating system for the brand.

It has five jobs:

1. Preserve product accuracy.
2. Establish one consistent brand position.
3. Define the public pages that should exist now, later, or only when data supports them.
4. Give copywriters and designers enough structure to create high-converting pages without inventing claims.
5. Prevent the public website from drifting back toward dummy statistics, fake proof, misleading marketplace language, or unsupported product promises.

### Source hierarchy

When sources conflict or future changes occur, use this order:

1. **Current working product behavior and approved business-owner information.**
2. **GetContractorz Public Website Foundation** - verified answers to the 32 business, positioning, trust, conversion, and content questions.
3. **GetContractorz Public Website Design System** - visual implementation and public-page design rules.
4. **This document** - derived strategy, organization, SEO/conversion architecture, and recommended information architecture.
5. Existing public copy - **not a source of truth unless re-verified.**

### Accuracy rule

Only claims supported by the current product, approved business information, or reliable platform data may be published. When information is unknown, the website should omit it or state only what is known. It should never fill a credibility gap with invented proof.

# Part I - Brand and Business Foundation

## 1. Brand definition

### 1.1 What GetContractorz is

GetContractorz is a **business-management platform for service businesses** operating in Canada and the United States. It connects the operational work surrounding clients and jobs inside one system, including:

- Client management.
- Team members/employees.
- Business services.
- Service-specific questionnaires.
- Terms and conditions.
- Personalized client services.
- Quotes.
- Assigned jobs.
- Before-and-after job images.
- Invoices.
- Business and banking information.
- Payouts.
- General dashboard information and analytics.
- A public Marketplace where visitors can browse registered businesses and access their public contact information.

### 1.2 What GetContractorz is not

The brand must not be positioned as:

- A construction company.
- A contractor that performs the listed services.
- A staffing company employing Marketplace businesses.
- A verification or screening service.
- A licensing authority.
- A quality-guarantee program.
- A lead-generation guarantee.
- A service-booking intermediary that chooses a provider for the customer.
- A traditional directory whose main product is listings.
- A marketplace that becomes a party to the service agreement.
- A paid subscription product with finalized public plans or pricing, because no such pricing model is currently approved.

### 1.3 Core product category

**Primary category:** Service-business management platform.

Useful secondary category language may include:

- Contractor management platform.
- Service business operations platform.
- Job and client management platform for service businesses.
- Business operations software for property-related service providers.

"CRM" may be used only where the context is specifically client management. It should not reduce GetContractorz to a CRM because the product also covers jobs, questionnaires, terms, quotes, invoices, images, team assignments, payouts, and Marketplace visibility.

### 1.4 Brand purpose

Help service businesses organize and manage the operational workflow around their customers and jobs, from client onboarding and service requirements to team assignment, work documentation, quoting, invoicing, and payout tracking.

### 1.5 Practical brand promise

The safest high-level promise is **organization and workflow centralization**, not guaranteed growth or outcomes.

Approved concise value proposition:

> **Run your service business from one organized workspace.**

This should function as a strategic message, not necessarily as the final homepage headline.

## 2. Market and audience hierarchy

### 2.1 Geographic scope

GetContractorz currently serves businesses in:

- Canada.
- United States.

Do not claim province-, state-, metro-, or city-level coverage unless the claim is backed by actual registered-business data.

### 2.2 Primary public audience

The primary audience is owners and managers of supported service businesses that need a simpler way to organize operations.

This includes businesses in construction, cleaning, repair, maintenance, moving, landscaping, and the other supported service categories.

### 2.3 Secondary public audience

People looking for one of the supported service providers through the Marketplace.

Their public-site need is discovery and direct contact, not a promise that GetContractorz has selected, verified, or endorsed the business.

### 2.4 Existing invited clients

Invited clients are not the primary acquisition audience, but the public website should clearly explain the client experience because they may land on public pages before or after receiving an invitation.

Their needs include:

- Understanding what GetContractorz is.
- Understanding why they received an invitation or magic link.
- Knowing what they can do in their account.
- Understanding that GetContractorz does not guarantee the service business.
- Knowing that there is currently no GetContractorz platform fee for them.

### 2.5 Employees

Employees are product users but not a primary public acquisition audience. Their workflow should be explained as part of "How It Works" or role-based product explanations rather than receiving a major SEO landing page at launch.

## 3. User and role model

GetContractorz has four registered roles plus a public visitor state.

| Role | Definition | Public-site relevance |
|---|---|---|
| User | Newly registered account that has not yet registered a business | Explains onboarding and the transition to Manager |
| Manager | Service-business owner or manager with access to the complete business workspace | Primary conversion audience |
| Employee | Team member who can view and complete assigned jobs | Product workflow and team-management proof |
| Client | Customer of a registered business with access to relevant service information/actions | Secondary informational audience |
| Marketplace visitor | Public user browsing registered businesses and contact information | Secondary acquisition/discovery audience |

### Role hierarchy for messaging

1. **Manager / service business** - dominant on the homepage and main product pages.
2. **Marketplace visitor** - prominent but separate discovery path.
3. **Client** - supported through a clear informational page and FAQ coverage.
4. **Employee** - explained inside product/process pages.
5. **User** - described mainly in registration/onboarding explanations.

## 4. Jobs to be done

### 4.1 Service-business owner / manager

When day-to-day operations are spread across disconnected tools or manual processes, the manager needs one place to organize:

- Clients.
- Services.
- Service requirements.
- Team members.
- Job assignments.
- Work documentation.
- Quotes.
- Invoices.
- Payouts.
- Business information.

The desired outcome is better operational visibility and organization. Do not translate this into unsupported claims about time saved, revenue increased, or errors eliminated unless real data later exists.

### 4.2 Employee

When a manager assigns work, the employee needs to see and complete the assigned job and document the work using before-and-after images.

### 4.3 Invited client

When receiving a service through a registered business, the client needs a clear place to:

- Access their account.
- View services created for them.
- Complete service questionnaires.
- Follow service-related email/magic-link actions.
- Access relevant quotes.
- Access relevant invoices.

### 4.4 Marketplace visitor

When searching for a service provider, the visitor needs to:

- Browse registered businesses.
- Understand their categories/location information where supplied.
- View public business information.
- Contact a selected business directly.

The relationship and service arrangement remain between the visitor and the selected business.

## 5. Problem landscape

### 5.1 Primary problem for service businesses

Service businesses may manage client information, job details, employees, requirements, photos, quotes, invoices, and payment-related information across separate systems, spreadsheets, messages, documents, or manual processes.

GetContractorz's role is to centralize the supported workflows into one organized platform.

### 5.2 Client-side problem

Service information can be fragmented between email, forms, quotes, invoices, and the business itself. GetContractorz gives invited clients a role-specific place to complete required questionnaires and access information relevant to their services.

### 5.3 Discovery problem

People seeking service providers need a straightforward way to find registered businesses and access their public contact information. The Marketplace supports this without representing those businesses as verified or endorsed.

---

## 6. Competitive and category positioning

### 6.1 Core differentiation

GetContractorz should not compete only on "finding contractors." Its stronger distinction is:

- The **core product is operational software for service businesses**.
- The **Marketplace is a complementary public discovery layer**.
- Registered businesses can manage the client/job workflow inside the same broader platform in which they are publicly discoverable.

### 6.2 Distinction from Google or a traditional directory

A search engine or directory mainly helps someone find a business. GetContractorz's main product is the business workspace used to manage service operations. The Marketplace adds discoverability to that operational platform.

### 6.3 Positioning statement

**For service businesses in Canada and the United States that need to organize clients, teams, jobs, documents, quotes, invoices, and payout-related workflows, GetContractorz is a service-business management platform that brings those operational activities into one connected workspace. Unlike a traditional directory, its core value is the business operations platform, while its Marketplace provides a separate way for the public to discover registered businesses and contact them directly.**

This is a strategic positioning statement, not final customer-facing copy.

### 6.4 Positioning hierarchy

Public messaging should generally move in this order:

1. **Business outcome category:** organized service-business operations.
2. **Mechanism:** one connected workspace.
3. **Concrete capabilities:** clients, team, services, questionnaires, jobs, quotes, invoices, payouts, work images.
4. **Secondary value:** Marketplace discoverability/direct contact.
5. **Low-friction conversion:** current free registration and access.
6. **Trust boundary:** no verification, endorsement, guaranteed leads, or guaranteed service outcomes.

## 9. Business registration and operating workflow

The verified standard business journey is:

1. A person registers and initially receives the User role.
2. The user completes the business-registration form in the Business tab.
3. The business is created and the role changes to Manager.
4. The Manager gains access to the business workspace.
5. The Manager selects the applicable supported service category or categories.
6. The Manager configures the business's services.
7. The Manager creates service questionnaires and terms and conditions.
8. The Manager adds clients and team members.
9. A personalized service is created for a client and assigned to an employee.
10. The client submits the required questionnaire.
11. The Manager reviews the submission and activates the service.
12. The assigned employee completes the job and uploads before-and-after images.
13. The Manager handles related quotes, invoices, payouts, and business reporting through the workspace.

### Copywriting implication

Do not force all 13 steps into the homepage. Use a short conversion-oriented version on high-level pages and the full workflow on the "How It Works" page.

---

## 10. Invited-client workflow

The verified standard invited-client journey is:

1. A business manager creates the client's record.
2. The client receives an email invitation.
3. The client follows the link to create or access the account.
4. The manager creates a personalized service for the client.
5. The client receives a magic link to the service questionnaire.
6. The client completes and submits the questionnaire.
7. The manager reviews the submission.
8. The manager changes the service from pending to active.
9. The client accesses the relevant parts of the platform, including applicable services, quotes, and invoices.

### Copywriting implication

Public copy should explain enough of this process to reduce confusion around invitations, questionnaires, and magic links, without turning every marketing page into product documentation.

---

## 11. Employee workflow

The verified employee-level role is narrower than the Manager role:

- Employees can view assigned jobs.
- Employees can complete assigned jobs.
- The workflow includes uploading before-and-after images.

Do not imply employees have the complete Manager workspace unless the product later changes.

---

## 12. Marketplace workflow

The verified Marketplace relationship is:

1. A visitor can browse registered businesses.
2. Public contact information can be accessed.
3. The visitor chooses which business to contact.
4. Communication and service arrangements happen directly between the visitor and the business.
5. GetContractorz does not select the business for the visitor.
6. GetContractorz does not guarantee the business, service quality, job completion, response, or outcome.

# Part VII - Voice and Copywriting Standards

## 23. Brand voice

### 23.1 Professional

Use polished, complete language that feels appropriate for a business owner managing customers, workers, documents, and money-related workflows.

### 23.2 Simple

Explain product actions using the same nouns users see in the product. Prefer "clients," "jobs," "quotes," "invoices," and "questionnaires" over abstract software jargon.

### 23.3 Helpful

Answer the question a visitor is likely asking at that moment. Do not force promotional copy into every sentence.

### 23.4 Confident

State verified capabilities directly. Avoid weakening true claims with excessive qualifiers.

### 23.5 Restrained

Do not use exaggerated transformation language to compensate for missing proof.

---

## 24. Recommended writing style

Use:

- Active voice.
- Short-to-medium sentences.
- Specific product nouns.
- Operational verbs: manage, add, assign, create, send, review, complete, access, upload, organize.
- Clear audience labels.
- Scannable headings.
- One idea per paragraph.
- Concrete feature-to-outcome connections.
- Direct CTA labels.

Avoid:

- "Revolutionary."
- "Game-changing."
- "Best-in-class."
- "Trusted by thousands" without data.
- "Guaranteed."
- "Verified professionals."
- "Never lose a lead again."
- "Skyrocket your business."
- "Save X hours" without evidence.
- "All-in-one" when it can imply unsupported breadth.
- Overly technical backend/product terminology.
- Empty phrases such as "innovative solutions designed for your success."

### 24.1 Tone by context

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Homepage/product | Confident, concise, operational |
| How It Works | Clear, instructional, reassuring |
| Marketplace | Neutral, useful, discovery-oriented |
| Client information | Helpful, calm, explanatory |
| FAQ | Direct, factual, non-defensive |
| Contact | Helpful, concise |
| Trust disclaimer | Calm, plain-language, non-alarmist |
| Legal | Precise, readable, formal enough for the purpose |

# Part X - SEO Strategy and Search Architecture

## 32. SEO objective

Build organic visibility around two real search-intent families without confusing them:

## 35. SEO page-quality rules

Every indexable page should have:

- One clear search intent.
- One semantic H1.
- Unique title metadata.
- Unique meta description.
- Useful body information.
- Internal links.
- A logical conversion action.
- Crawlable server-rendered core content.
- Canonical URL.
- Accurate structured data only where applicable.
- No unsupported keyword-driven claims.

### Thin-page rule

Do not index pages that exist only because a filter combination can generate a URL.

Examples to avoid:

- Empty city pages.
- Near-identical category + city permutations.
- Pages with only a category title and a list count of zero.
- Duplicate Marketplace filter states.
- Business profiles with insufficient public information.

If not, keep the category in the index grid without creating a thin standalone page.

---

## 80. Location-page publishing rule

A location page should require:

- Real registered businesses in that location.
- A stable definition of location/service area.
- Enough listings or unique useful data to justify a page.
- Accurate title/H1 without implying total market coverage.
- Unique context.
- Non-duplicative value.

Do not publish a location page just because a keyword has volume.

---

## 81. Business-profile publishing rule

A Marketplace business profile should preferably include enough public business-supplied information to be useful, such as:

- Business name.
- Category/categories.
- Location/service area.
- Business description.
- Public contact information.

If the profile is too thin, consider noindex until it becomes useful.

# Part XXII - Visual and Technical Quality Requirements

## 82. Accessibility

Target WCAG 2.2 AA.

Required public-page behavior includes:

- Semantic landmarks.
- Skip-to-content link.
- One H1.
- Logical headings.
- Visible keyboard focus.
- Sufficient contrast.
- 44x44px minimum pointer targets.
- Accessible accordions.
- Accessible mobile navigation.
- Form labels and errors.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Accurate alt text for meaningful imagery.
- Decorative imagery hidden from assistive technology.

---

## 83. Performance and crawlability

- Server-render essential copy.
- Do not hide H1s behind animation.
- Do not use images for text headings.
- Reserve image dimensions.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold imagery.
- Optimize decorative assets.
- Avoid full-screen loading placeholders.
- Keep DOM order aligned with reading order.
- Use canonical URLs.
- Ensure CTAs and internal links point to real destinations.

---

## 84. Responsive design

Key breakpoints:

- Mobile: base.
- Tablet: 768px+.
- Desktop: 1024px+.
- Wide: 1280px+ with capped content width.

Core behavior:

- 1 -> 2 -> 3/4 column grids where appropriate.
- Mobile gutters around 24px.
- Tablet gutters around 64px.
- Desktop gutters around 128px within capped content.
- No horizontal scrolling.
- Do not rely on hover for essential information.

# Part XXIV - Final Brand and Website Discipline

## 85. Brand operating principle

GetContractorz should earn confidence through **clarity, product specificity, and operational usefulness**, not through exaggerated proof.

## 86. Website operating principle

Every public page should answer three questions:

1. **Who is this page for?**
2. **What verified problem or task does it address?**
3. **What is the single most appropriate next action?**

If a section cannot answer one of those questions or provide real proof/context, it should probably not exist.

## 87. SEO operating principle

Create pages because a distinct user intent deserves a distinct useful answer, not because a keyword variation exists.

## 88. Conversion operating principle

Reduce friction by making the product understandable, the current-free-access model clear, the supported categories visible, and the next action obvious.

## 89. Trust operating principle

Never turn absence of verification, testimonials, statistics, or guarantees into invented trust language. Neutral accuracy is part of the brand.

## 90. Design operating principle

Use the established design grammar:

**Soft-gray space, navy structure, orange priority, MODERNIZ confidence, rounded practical components, restrained geometric decoration, and clear responsive grids.**

## 91. Definition of done for future public copy

A page is ready for publication when:

- Its audience and search intent are defined.
- Its primary conversion is defined.
- Every product claim is verified.
- Unsupported proof is absent.
- Marketplace trust boundaries are accurate.
- The page uses the shared visual system.
- It has one H1 and unique metadata.
- It links logically into the rest of the site.
- It does not duplicate another page's intent.
- It works on mobile and desktop.
- It meets accessibility requirements.
- It is crawlable where indexing is intended.
- Its legal/privacy statements match approved policies.
- Its CTA leads to a real working destination.
- The page still makes sense if no testimonials, logos, or statistics are available.
